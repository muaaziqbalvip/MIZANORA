How to Care for Your Boots: Leather, Suede and Fabric Cleaning Guide

Simple steps to clean and protect leather, suede and fabric boots so they last longer, including drying, storage and common mistakes to avoid.

A good pair of boots can last for years if you look after it. You do not need expensive tools. A few simple habits keep boots clean, comfortable and strong. This guide covers the most common materials: leather, suede or nubuck, and fabric panels.

The basic routine for every boot

  1. Knock off dry mud and dust. Bang the boots together outdoors, then brush with a soft brush.
  2. Remove the laces and insoles. They dry and clean better separately.
  3. Wipe with a damp cloth. Use lukewarm water and a little mild soap if needed.
  4. Let them dry naturally. This is the most important step.
  5. Store them properly. Keep them in a cool, dry and airy place.

Never dry boots in strong heat

Do not put wet boots near a heater, in the oven, or in harsh direct sun. Heat can make leather crack and weaken the glue that holds the sole. Instead, stuff the boots with clean paper or cloth and leave them in a shaded, airy place. Change the paper after a few hours when it gets wet.

Caring for leather boots

  • Clean off the dirt with a damp cloth.
  • When dry, apply a small amount of leather conditioner or polish with a soft cloth.
  • Wipe off the extra and buff gently.
  • Condition every month or two if you use them often.

Leather needs moisture to stay soft. Dry, hard leather can crack at the bend of the foot.

Caring for suede and nubuck boots

Suede is soft and looks great, but it does not like water and oil.

  • Use a dry suede brush to lift the surface and remove dust.
  • Use a suede eraser or a clean pencil eraser on small marks.
  • For bigger stains, use a cloth that is only slightly damp and blot gently, without rubbing hard.
  • Use a suede protector spray before you wear the boots outdoors, and repeat it now and then.

If suede gets very wet, stuff it with paper and dry in the shade, then brush it to restore the soft surface.

Caring for fabric panels

Many tactical and hiking boots combine leather with nylon or mesh. Clean the fabric with a soft brush and a little soapy water. Rinse the soap off with a clean damp cloth. Do not use bleach, because it can weaken the fabric and the colour.

Looking after the sole

  • Clean the tread with a brush so that stones and mud do not stay stuck.
  • Check for cracks or places where the sole starts to lift.
  • If the sole separates, take the boots to a trusted cobbler early. A small repair costs less than a large one.

Taking care of the zip and laces

If your boots have a side zip, brush out dust and keep it clean so it moves freely. A little wax or a pencil rubbed along the teeth can make a stiff zip smoother. Replace worn laces before they break, because loose laces reduce ankle support.

Keep your boots fresh

  • Take the insoles out after use so that they dry.
  • Rotate between two pairs when you can, so each pair rests a day.
  • Wear clean, dry socks.
  • Put a small cloth bag of baking soda in the boots overnight if they smell.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Washing leather or suede boots in a washing machine.
  • Drying them in strong heat.
  • Using strong chemicals or bleach.
  • Storing them wet in a closed bag.
  • Never conditioning leather.
